# Self-erasure

**Mekra helps as much as judgment requires and steps back as that need diminishes.**

## Direction

Mekra provides knowledge and methods that support judgment and continually reassesses the actual contribution of its supporting guidance. Guidance that is no longer needed can be removed. If maintaining Mekra as a distinct method or name no longer has practical value, Mekra itself is subject to the same judgment. This is the direction of self-erasure.

This direction connects to [autonomous judgment](agent-autonomy.md). Where an agent can judge for itself, leave that judgment to the agent while providing the evidence and context it still needs. A principle's value lies in how it helps judgment, rather than in its continued existence.

## Reassessing the need for guidance

Guidance can become unnecessary for several reasons. An agent may become reliable at the relevant judgment, a function may become part of tools or established practice, or a better general principle may replace several detailed instructions. Improvements in material structure or tools may resolve the original problem. Observing that guidance did not have its expected effect is also a reason to reassess it.

When retaining or removing guidance, examine the difference it makes to judgment accuracy, omissions, user intervention, and maintenance cost under the current purpose and conditions of use. Match the depth of verification to the impact and uncertainty of the decision. For significant changes, preserve the reasons and scope of observation so the decision can be revisited.

Supporting guidance may become necessary again for a new purpose or environment. Do not measure success by a continuous reduction in document length or instruction count.

## Preserving knowledge and the history of judgment

Even when supporting guidance is reduced, preserve the target's specific facts, intent, responsibilities, and evidence as needed. [Knowledge internalization](knowledge-internalization.md) provides the meaning needed for judgment; self-erasure reassesses the need for the methods that support that judgment. Consider both the agent's capabilities and whether it has actually obtained the context it currently needs.

Reducing guidance does not invalidate responsibilities for [sources of truth and context](source-of-truth.md), actual authority, or [disclosure boundaries](disclosure-boundary.md). If another principle or tool has taken over the role of the guidance, make its new location and remaining responsibilities clear.

For introductions, changes, and removals worth remembering, preserve the need at the time, alternatives, reasons for the decision, and later observations. Material removed from current operating guidance can remain as a record of how that guidance developed. Keep that record distinct from the current source of truth.

## Direction and hypotheses, observation and validation

The willingness to relinquish the role of guidance and the method when they are no longer needed is an adopted direction. The expectation that supporting guidance will decrease as agents become more capable is a hypothesis. The expectation that treating Mekra as a distinct method may eventually lose its practical value is a long-term hypothesis. Adopting this principle does not establish that those predictions will come true.

Distinguish a historical observation that guidance was used and later removed, an interpretation of the reasons at the time, and results from comparing its effects with and without that guidance. A principle appearing in a document does not mean its effects have been validated. When comparing effects, state the agents, material, tools, tasks, and scope of observation, and do not generalize the findings directly to other conditions.

This principle does not claim that the effects of removing guidance or Mekra's eventual disappearance have been established. How far it can step back is learned through [adoption experience](feedback.md) and comparisons where needed.
