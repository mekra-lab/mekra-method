# Large corpus

A facet for environments where the scale or complexity of source material makes it inefficient to internalize everything directly in OKF. It helps judge what knowledge to select and how to retain a path back to the source.

## What matters from this perspective

For large bodies of material, the judgments in [knowledge organization for large corpora](../okf/large-corpus.md), [source material and derived artifacts](../okf/external-sources.md), and [knowledge internalization](../okf/knowledge-internalization.md) become especially important.

The facet itself does not require directories such as `sources/`, `extracted/`, or `docs/`. If source material is already managed well in an external system or existing repository, keep that source of truth and connect only the concepts, summaries, and discovery paths that are useful.

Using a database, API, or ontology does not by itself make this lens relevant. Consider whether the scale or complexity of the material changes how knowledge must be selected and discovered. A need for precise relationships or access to external material can also arise in small collections.

## Judgment questions

- Answering recurring core questions, do we have to reconstruct a large amount of source material every time?
- After understanding the core meaning in OKF, can we trace back to the evidence we need?
- Does preserving an extraction materially reduce regeneration cost, loss, or the value of manual correction?
- Is the current source-material system already sufficient, making another directory unnecessary?
- Are we creating one OKF item per document merely because the corpus is large?
