# Knowledge-centered

A facet for environments where knowledge itself is the primary output. More important than folder names or storage locations is how responsibility is divided among source material, derived artifacts, and internalized knowledge.

## What matters from this perspective

- Do not copy all source material into OKF. Internalize concepts, rules, and relationships that are repeatedly useful for understanding and judgment.
- Source material, extracted artifacts, and OKF knowledge may contain overlapping information while carrying different responsibilities. Prefer the existing storage system when one is already appropriate.
- Procedures such as collection, OCR, or ingestion belong in an existing README, work document, or another suitable location when managing them separately has practical value. Reusable meaning such as material-selection criteria or evidence judgment may become OKF knowledge.
- Directories such as `raw/`, `sources/`, `extracted/`, or `docs/` are implementation choices, not facet requirements.

## Judgment questions

- What is the source of truth for the source material, and what is interpretation or a derived artifact?
- What knowledge should remain understandable without rereading the source material every time?
- Would a separate storage layer or retained extraction materially improve reuse, traceability, or regeneration cost?
- Is keeping the existing storage location and tools better than creating a new structure?
